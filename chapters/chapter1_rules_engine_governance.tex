\chapter{The Rules Engine: Game Governance, Timers, and Attempt Limits}

\begin{tcolorbox}[storybox, title={The Rules Engine in Brief}]
Before a single question appears on a player's phone screen, the Quizard backend consults its **Rules Engine**. The Rules Engine acts as the tournament constitution: it dynamically sets how many questions each player receives (e.g., 60 questions), how many seconds they have to answer them (e.g., 180 seconds / 3 minutes), how many attempts they are allowed per day (e.g., exactly 2 rounds per day), and what reward prizes are at stake.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{Why Dynamic Rules Matter}
In traditional mobile games, quiz rules and timers are often hardcoded directly into the phone app. If tournament organizers want to change a quiz duration from 3 minutes to 2 minutes for a special holiday tournament, they would normally have to build and publish a brand new app update to the Google Play Store and Apple App Store.

Quizard solves this through its **Backend-Driven Rules Engine** (mapped in Django to \texttt{apps.cms.models.Rules} and in .NET to the \texttt{Rule} domain entity). Every aspect of gameplay is determined on the server in real time:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Allocated Time (\texttt{allocated\_time}):} Total gameplay window in seconds (e.g., $180\text{ seconds} = 3\text{ minutes}$).
    \item \textbf{Question Set Count (\texttt{question\_set}):} Total questions served per quiz round (e.g., 60 questions).
    \item \textbf{Daily Round Limit (\texttt{round}):} Maximum allowed plays per calendar day (e.g., 2 attempts per day).
    \item \textbf{Passing Score (\texttt{passing\_score}):} Minimum correct answers required to qualify for daily rewards (e.g., 20 correct answers).
    \item \textbf{Category Distribution (\texttt{category}):} Array of approved topic IDs (General Knowledge, BCS Preparation, Sports, Science, Islamic History).
    \item \textbf{Reward Prize Pool (\texttt{winningPrize}):} Daily, weekly, or special event cash prizes (e.g., 5,000 BDT to 25,000 BDT).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Enforcing the Daily Attempt Cap}
Rahim knows he has **2 attempts per day** for this tournament. 

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{First Attempt at 09:00 AM:} Rahim plays his first round. He answers 60 rapid questions, scoring \textbf{54 correct} in \textbf{145 seconds}.
    \item \textbf{Second Attempt at 02:00 PM:} Seeking to beat his score, Rahim plays his second round. He scores \textbf{58 correct} in \textbf{140 seconds}.
    \item \textbf{Third Attempt at 06:00 PM (Blocked):} When Rahim attempts to start a third round, the backend counts his completed records for the day:
    $$\text{Daily Attempt Count} = 2 \ge \text{Allocated Rounds } (2)$$
    The backend returns an informative HTTP 403 response: \texttt{"Daily round limit reached. Please return tomorrow at 12:00 AM for fresh attempts."}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{tcolorbox}[summarybox, title={Chapter Key Takeaways}]
\begin{itemize}
    \item Quizard decouples gameplay rules from client applications, empowering administrators to configure tournament parameters instantly on the server.
    \item A standard rapid tournament serves **60 questions** within an **allocated time of 180 seconds** (3 minutes).
    \item The backend strictly validates daily attempt limits (e.g., 2 rounds per day), preventing brute-force spamming while maintaining competitive fairness across Bangladesh.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}
